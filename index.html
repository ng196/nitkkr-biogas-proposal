1. Background / problem

Cooking fuel on campus is largely LPG, a fossil fuel with climate and supply-cost exposure. Campus wet waste — hostel mess and canteen food waste, garden litter, STP sludge — leaves campus or is handled off-site instead of being recovered as energy and manure. Anaerobic digestion converts segregated organic waste into biogas for cooking and digestate for campus use.

2. Potential of biogas / national schemes

  • Biogas from institutional organic waste is supported under national programmes: GOBARdhan (Galvanizing Organic Bio-Agro Resources Dhan) and the MNRE Biogas Programme (Annex B).
  • A compressed bio-gas (CBG) offtake path exists under MoPNG’s SATAT scheme — kept as a variation shell in Annex E.
  • Scheme fit for NITKKR: empty — Compliance.

Annex E. SATAT scheme (CBG offtake variation)

Parallel path: campus cooking-gas pilot vs compressed bio-gas sold under MoPNG’s offtake ecosystem. Scheme facts from MoPNG; our fit stays empty.

What SATAT is (MoPNG)

  • Full name: Sustainable Alternative Towards Affordable Transportation.
  • Launched 1 October 2018 by the Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas (MoPNG).
  • Aim: an ecosystem for Compressed Bio Gas (CBG) from waste / biomass, marketed by Oil Marketing Companies as an alternative transport fuel.
  • Under SATAT, Oil & Gas Marketing Companies (IOCL, BPCL, HPCL, GAIL, IGL) invited Expressions of Interest to procure CBG from entrepreneurs.
  • MoPNG notes: long-term offtake agreements at an assured price; FOM (Fermented Organic Manure) notified under FCO 1985 (13 July 2020); CBG under Priority Sector Lending (RBI, 4.9.2020); Haryana & Punjab State Level Committees for SATAT; MNRE CFA extension noted historically for FY 2020-21 (no CFA ₹ banked here).
  • Sources: MoPNG — Compressed Bio Gas · PIB launch release
